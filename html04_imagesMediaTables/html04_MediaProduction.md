# Lesson 04 Media Production: The 20-Second Lego Promo

## What you are making

A **20-second video ad for your website**, acted out by Lego figures.

- The Lego figures are the actors. You are not on camera.
- You write a script first and time it. If it runs over 20 seconds, cut words.
- You record the voices in **Audacity** with a real microphone.
- You shoot the pictures (or short video clips) of the Lego figures with a phone.
- You put the pictures, the voices, and background music together in **Canva** and export an MP4.
- The MP4 goes in your site's `media` folder and gets embedded with a `<video>` tag on Wednesday (Day 2 of the DIY task).

It does not matter what your site is about. A site about fishing, a site about a video game, a site about your dog: the Lego figures sell it.

**Hard limits:**
- 20 seconds max. Canva shows the length at the bottom of the screen.
- File under 10 MB.
- No copyrighted music. Use Canva's built-in music.
- School-appropriate. Your teacher and your parents could watch it.

## Step 1: Write and time the script

Do this on paper before you touch a phone, a mic, or Canva. Nobody records until the script is timed.

**The shape of a 20-second ad:**

| Seconds | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | The problem or the hook | "Ugh, I can never find a good trail near Medina." |
| 4–14 | The site and what's on it (2 or 3 things) | "Check out MedinaTrails.com. Maps, photos, and a difficulty rating for every trail." |
| 14–20 | The closing line with the site name | "MedinaTrails.com. Go outside already." |

**Rules:**
- Two characters max. One is fine.
- About 45 words total. People talk at 2 to 3 words per second. 50 words will not fit.
- Say the site name at least once, at the end.

**Time it:** read the script out loud at normal speed while a partner times you with a phone. Do it three times. If any read is over 17 seconds, cut words. You need a few seconds of room for the music to start and end.

**Write the shot list** on the same paper: a line for each picture you will take. For a 20-second video you need **20 to 40 pictures** (see Step 4).

Show your teacher the timed script and shot list before you move on.

## Step 2: Record the voices in Audacity

Recording happens at the Mac with the microphones. **Sign up for a slot on the board** as soon as your script is approved. Slots are 4 minutes. If you are not ready when your slot comes up, you go to the end of the list. **While you wait your turn, go shoot your pictures (Step 4).**

### At the recording station

1. Open **Audacity**.
2. At the top of the window, find the **microphone dropdown** (next to the microphone icon). Pick the condenser mic, not the Mac's built-in mic. Ask if you are not sure which name is the mic.
3. Sit about a hand's width (6 to 8 inches) from the mic. Talk **across** it slightly, not straight into it, so your P and B sounds don't pop.
4. Click the **Record** button (red circle) and read one line. Click **Stop** (black square). Look at the waveform:
   - Peaks should reach about **halfway to two-thirds** of the track height.
   - If the waveform is tiny, move closer or turn up the mic gain.
   - If it's a solid block that hits the top, you are too loud or too close. Delete it and try again.
5. When the level looks right, click at the start of the track, press **Record**, and read the whole script. Leave one second of silence before you start and after you finish. Press **Stop**.
6. Mess up a line? Keep going. Just pause, say the line again, and keep reading. You will cut the bad take out.
7. Two characters? Record each character's lines all the way through, then do the other voice. Or have a partner do the second voice. Both voices go in the same recording.

### Clean it up (5 minutes, no more)

- **Cut a mistake:** click and drag across the bad part of the waveform to select it, then press **Delete**.
- **Trim the ends:** select the dead air at the very start, Delete. Same at the end (leave about half a second).
- **Even out the volume:** Select All (**Cmd+A**), then **Effect > Volume and Compression > Normalize**, click Apply. This makes the loud parts a standard level.
- **Listen once** from the start (press **Space**). If it sounds fine, you're done. Do not spend time on effects.

### Export

1. **File > Export Audio**.
2. File name: `promo-voice-lastname` (your last name, no spaces).
3. Format: **MP3**. Leave the quality setting alone.
4. Save it to the **Desktop** for now.
5. Also **File > Save Project** with the same name, in case you need to come back to it.

## Step 3: Get the audio file to your computer

The Mac is not on your account, so the file travels by thumb drive.

1. Plug the class thumb drive into the Mac.
2. Drag `promo-voice-lastname.mp3` from the Desktop onto the thumb drive icon.

4. Plug it into your computer and copy the file into your site's `media` folder.

## Step 4: Shoot the Lego pictures

You are making a **stop-motion** video: a series of still pictures that play fast enough to look like movement. Real stop motion uses 12 pictures per second. You do not have time for that. You will use **2 pictures per second**, so 20 seconds needs **about 40 pictures**. That is enough to look animated and not enough to take all day.

Do the math from your script: if a character talks for 5 seconds, that scene needs about 10 pictures.

### Setup

- Build a small set on a desk: a plain background (a sheet of paper, a folder, a book cover), the figures, and one or two props. Keep it simple.
- Put the phone somewhere it **will not move** between shots. Lean it against a stack of books, tape it to the desk, or have a partner hold it against something solid. A moving camera ruins stop motion.
- Turn on the desk lamp if you have one and keep the overhead light steady. Do not use flash.
- Shoot in **landscape** (phone sideways). Every picture in landscape.

### Shooting

1. Set the figures in the starting pose. Take a picture.
2. Move the figure a **tiny** bit (a few millimeters, a slight arm turn, a small step). Take a picture.
3. Repeat. Small moves look smooth. Big moves look like teleporting.
4. Follow your shot list. When the script says the character points at the sign, you need 4 or 5 pictures of the arm coming up.
5. Take a few extra pictures of the last pose. You need something to hold on screen while the site name is said.

**Shortcut option:** instead of pictures, shoot a **short video clip** of you moving the figure by hand, then speed it up or trim it in Canva. Less stop-motion feel, but faster. Talk to your teacher before you pick this.

**Optional:** the free app **Stop Motion Studio** (iPhone and Android) shoots real stop motion and exports one video file. If you already know it, use it and skip to Step 5 with a video instead of pictures.

### Getting the pictures to Canva

Easiest way: install the **Canva app** on your phone, sign in with your school Canva account, tap **Uploads**, and upload the pictures from the phone. They show up in Canva on your computer right away.

Backup: email the pictures to yourself, or use the thumb drive.

## Step 5: Put it together in Canva

1. In Canva, click **Create a design**, search for **Video**, and pick the landscape one (1920 x 1080).
2. **Uploads** on the left: upload your voice MP3 (from Step 3) and all your pictures, if they aren't there already.
3. **Add the voice first.** Drag the MP3 onto the timeline at the bottom. Your video length is now set by the voice. Everything else fits to it.
4. **Add the pictures.** Drag the first picture onto page 1 and stretch it to fill the page. Add a new page for each picture. Faster way: select all the pictures in Uploads and drag them in as a batch, then set each page to fill.
5. **Set the timing.** Click a page's thumbnail in the timeline, then click the **clock icon** (or **Edit timing**) and set it to **0.5 seconds**. Select all pages first (click one, Shift+click the last) to set them all at once.
6. **Line up the pictures with the words.** Press play. When the character is supposed to be talking, their pictures should be on screen. Drag pages left or right in the timeline to fix it.
7. **Add text.** At minimum: the **site name** on the last few pages. Big, readable, high contrast. One font.
8. **Add music.** **Elements > Audio** (or the **Audio** tab). Pick something from Canva's library. Drag it to the timeline under the voice. Click the music track, click the **volume icon**, and turn it down to about **15 to 20%** so the voice wins. Trim the music to the video length.
9. **Check the length** at the bottom of the screen. **20.0 seconds or less.** If it's over, cut pictures, not words (you already timed the words).
10. Play it all the way through **twice**. Fix anything that bugs you. Then stop. It's a 20-second ad, not a movie.

## Step 6: Export and add it to your site

### Export from Canva

1. **Share** (top right) **> Download**.
2. File type: **MP4 Video**.
3. Quality/size: **720p**, not 1080p. 1080p makes a file too big for GitHub.
4. Download it. Rename it `promo-lastname.mp4`.
5. **Check the file size.** Right-click the file, Properties (Windows) or Get Info (Mac). It should be **under 10 MB**. If it isn't, export again at a lower quality or trim it.

### Add it to your site

1. Copy `promo-lastname.mp4` into your site's `media` folder.
2. On the page you picked for your media section, add:

```html
<section id="promo">
  <h2>Watch the Promo</h2>
  <video width="640" height="360" controls>
    <source src="media/promo-lastname.mp4" type="video/mp4">
    Your browser does not support HTML5 video.
  </video>
</section>
```

3. Open the page in your browser. Play it. Sound on.
4. Commit and push. Check on github.com that the `media` folder and the MP4 are there.

The MP4 is the only format you need. Every current browser plays it.

## If something goes wrong

**Audacity shows a flat line when I record.** The wrong input is selected. Check the microphone dropdown. On a Mac, also check **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone** has Audacity turned on.

**My voice sounds far away or echoey.** Too far from the mic. Get to a hand's width.

**Pops on P and B sounds.** Talk across the mic, not into it, or move slightly to the side.

**The stop motion looks jumpy.** Your moves between pictures were too big, or the camera moved. Small moves. Anchor the phone.

**Pictures are sideways in Canva.** They were shot in portrait. Rotate them in Canva (select, then the rotate handle), or reshoot in landscape.

**Canva says the video is over 20 seconds.** Delete pictures. The voice was already timed under 17. Do not cut the voice.

**The MP4 is over 10 MB.** Export again at 720p. Still too big? Your video is probably over 20 seconds. Check.

**The video plays but there's no sound.** In Canva, the voice track volume is at 0, or you deleted it. Go back and check the timeline. Also check your computer isn't muted.

**The `<video>` shows a broken player on my site.** Path problem. The file name in `src` has to match the real file name exactly, including capital letters and `.mp4`. And the file has to be in a folder named `media` next to the HTML file.

## Part 2 — Write Your Client Questions

Write at least **15** questions total that you would ask a customer. Sort your questions under the six headings in the table below. Put at least two questions under each heading.

| Heading | What you are trying to find out |
|---|---|
| **The Business** | What the business does and who runs it |
| **The Audience** | Who will visit the site and what they should do there (call, buy, sign up, visit the store) |
| **The Content** | What pages are needed, and who provides the text and photos |
| **Branding** | Whether the business already has a logo, colors, and fonts, or needs them created (see [Branding Hints](#branding-hints)) |
| **The Look** | The style the client wants, and websites they like or dislike |
| **The Project** | The deadline, who updates the site later, and where it will be published |

**Tip:** Avoid yes/no questions when you can. "What should a visitor do first on your site?" gets a better answer than "Do you want a contact page?"

## Part 3 — Make a Client Intake Form

Turn your questions into a form a client could fill out before your first meeting.

**Rules for the form:**

- Keep the wording simple. The client may not know web terms.
- Do not use words like "HTML," "hosting," or "SVG" unless you explain them in plain English.
- Leave space for the client's answers.
- Use the same six headings from Part 2, with a list or table for the questions.

**Example format:**

```markdown
### About Your Business

**1. What is the name of your business?**

Answer: ____________________

**2. In one or two sentences, what does your business do?**

Answer: ____________________
```

## Branding Hints

Branding is how a business looks everywhere it shows up: its logo, colors, fonts, and style. Your website must match it. A client will fall into one of two situations.

### Situation 1: The client already has branding

Ask for the files and details you will need:

- **Logo files.** Ask for the logo as an **SVG** file if they have one. An SVG is made of shapes instead of pixels, so it stays sharp at any size. A PNG is the next best choice. A blurry JPG copied from Facebook is hard to work with.
- **Exact colors.** Ask for the color codes (for example, `#1E6FD9`). These are the same hex codes you use in CSS.
- **Fonts.** Ask which fonts they use on signs, menus, or business cards.
- **Other materials.** Ask for business cards, flyers, or menus that show the style.

### Situation 2: The client is starting from scratch

You may need to help create a simple logo and color set. Ask questions such as:

- What three words describe how you want customers to feel about your business?
- Are there colors you love or colors you want to avoid?
- Are there logos from other businesses that you like? What do you like about them?
- Should the logo include a picture, just the business name, or both?

Things to start thinking about (you do not need to build these yet):

- A logo made as an **SVG** can be used on the website, on a sign, and as a small browser tab icon without losing quality.
- A small **color palette** (two or three main colors) keeps a site looking consistent.

### Photos and media

Ask every client about images:

- Who will provide the photos? The client, a photographer, or free stock photo sites?
- Does the client own the rights to use the photos online?
- Are there any videos to include?
